## Why Sparkly

JWST, aided by strong gravitational lensing, has begun to resolve individual star clusters in galaxies seen only a few hundred million years after the Big Bang. Systems such as the Sparkler (z = 1.38), the Firefly Sparkle (z = 8.3) and the Cosmic Gems arc (z = 9.6) show compact, massive clusters that may be the progenitors of today's globular clusters. Making sense of these objects requires communities that rarely meet: observers of lensed high-redshift galaxies, researchers studying globular and young star clusters in the local Universe, and the simulators and theorists who model how clusters form, evolve and shape their host galaxies.

Sparkly is a monthly online meeting series built to bring these communities together.

## Three links

The series is organized around three connections:

1. **Local to early Universe.** From the globular cluster systems of the Milky Way and nearby galaxies to the proto-globular clusters now seen at cosmic dawn.
2. **Star clusters to their host galaxies.** How cluster populations trace the assembly of galaxies and their dark matter haloes, and the scaling relations that connect them.
3. **Observations to simulations and theory.** Confronting JWST measurements of cluster masses, sizes, ages and chemistry with cosmological simulations, cluster-scale simulations and analytic models.

## Themes

- High-redshift clusters in lensed galaxies
- Local globular and young star clusters
- Galaxy scaling relations and the globular cluster to halo mass relation
- Simulations and theory of cluster formation, survival and disruption
- Chemical abundances and the origin of multiple populations
- Star clusters and reionization
- Star clusters as hosts of intermediate-mass black holes

## Format

A monthly Zoom talk, usually one hour, with time for discussion. The organizers also meet between talks to develop collaborative projects. Recordings and slides are posted on the [Meetings]({{ '/meetings/' | relative_url }}) page when speakers agree.
